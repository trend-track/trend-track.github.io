Trend Track Review (2026): Features, Limits and Pricing

Trend Track, written TrendTrack on the site itself, is a spy stack for ecommerce: shops, ad libraries, advertisers and email flows under one login.

If you only read one paragraphThis suits dropshippers, media buyers and DTC operators who spend their week looking at other people's stores and want that research in one tab instead of nine. It is a poor fit for anyone selling a service or software product, because the entire dataset is built around shops, catalogues and ecommerce ads. The caveat is scope creep: eleven features sounds generous until you notice you use two of them, and the plan you need is priced for all eleven. Reachara is the narrower option if competitor ad monitoring and creator discovery are the only parts you were ever going to open.

What Trend Track is actually for

The site describes a research stack rather than a single tool. Shops lets you find fast-growing stores and filter them; Ads Library collects winning ads with their context; Advertisers goes the other way, from high-spending accounts back to the shops they promote. On top of that sit Shop Analytics, which pulls traffic, ads, products, reviews and social growth into one view of a store, and Similar Shops for finding businesses that look like yours. Read those descriptions together and the intent is clear: pick any store on the internet and reconstruct how it gets its customers. That is genuinely useful if your job is choosing products or angles. It is close to useless if your product is not something sold from a catalogue.

Email flows are the underrated part

Two features cover a channel most competitor tools ignore entirely. Email Library lets you browse and save emails, and Shop Emails shows a specific store's flows, campaigns and sending patterns. That combination answers questions ad spy tools cannot: how many days after a purchase the upsell lands, what a winning abandoned-cart sequence looks like, how often a brand emails before customers start unsubscribing. Email is where ecommerce margin quietly lives, and reference material for it is much harder to find than ad creative. If you are evaluating the platform and you already have a favourite ad library, this is the part to test first, because it is the piece least likely to duplicate something you pay for already.

Brandtracker, folders and the MCP

Three smaller features change how the platform fits a routine. Brandtracker follows chosen shops in real time across ads, emails, traffic and products, which turns one-off research into monitoring. Folders save ads, emails and shops into collections, which matters more than it sounds once a team is sharing swipe files. The MCP entry is the notable one: it connects Claude, ChatGPT and other assistants to pull stores, ads, advertisers and emails directly, so the data can feed an agent instead of a human tab. If you already run assistants over your marketing data, that connection is a real differentiator and worth weighing against tools that keep everything locked behind their own interface.

Free tools tell you the data quality

Before paying anything, use the public utilities. The site lists an ecommerce competitor analysis that returns estimated traffic, active ads, catalogue size, Trustpilot rating, tech stack and best sellers for any store; a competitor ads finder covering Meta, TikTok and Google activity; a winning product finder that surfaces ten products a day; a ROAS break-even calculator; and a free Chrome extension. Run the first one against a store whose real numbers you know, perhaps your own or a client's. Estimated traffic figures in this category vary wildly between vendors, and five minutes with a store you can verify tells you more about whether the paid plan is worth it than any review, including this one.

Pricing and the narrower alternative

What you can plan for is the shape of the decision. Suites like this bill for the whole stack, and most buyers genuinely use two or three parts. Write down which features you would open weekly before you look at the tiers, then see whether the cheapest plan covering those is competitive. Four things worth knowing

Built for ecommerce only

Shops, catalogues, product pages and ecommerce ad accounts are the dataset. A SaaS or agency buyer will find most features pointing at objects their business does not have.

Email libraries included

Browsing other brands' campaigns and flows sits alongside the ad tooling, which is unusual. It is the feature most likely to be new to you if you already own an ad spy tool.

Assistants can query it

An MCP connection is listed for pulling stores, ads, advertisers and emails into Claude, ChatGPT and similar tools, rather than reading everything inside the dashboard.

Public tools before you pay

Competitor analysis, an ads finder, a product finder and a ROAS calculator are offered free. Test them against a store whose real numbers you already know.

A sensible order to try it

  1. Audit a store you know
    Run the free competitor analysis on your own shop or a client's. Compare the estimated traffic and ad counts against your real analytics to see how far the estimates drift.
  2. List the features you would open
    Write down which of the eleven features you would actually use in a normal week. Two is common. That list, not the feature grid, decides which plan is worth paying for.
  3. Pull one competitor end to end
    Take a single rival and work through their ads, emails and products in one sitting. If you finish with an angle you would not have found alone, the subscription pays for itself.
  4. Price the parts separately
    Compare the plan against narrower tools for the two features you named. Reachara covers competitor ads and creators on its own, so check whether the suite premium buys anything you need.

Questions buyers ask

Is Trend Track worth it for dropshipping?

It is aimed squarely at that use case, with a dedicated section for dropshippers and a daily winning product finder. Whether it pays back depends on volume: if you test products every week, better shortlists compound quickly. If you launch a store twice a year, the same research can be done manually for free.

How much does it cost?

The pages checked for this review do not display prices, so any figure quoted by a third party should be treated as unverified. Pricing is published on the official pricing page and changes periodically, so read it there and confirm which features each tier unlocks before comparing plans.

Does it work for SaaS or agencies?

Partly. Agencies get a use case of their own on the site for finding ecommerce clients, which is a lead-generation angle rather than a research one. If you sell software, most of the dataset describes objects you do not have, and a tool focused on ads and creators will fit better.

What does the MCP feature do?

It connects assistants such as Claude and ChatGPT to the platform so they can pull stores, ads, advertisers and emails directly. In practice that means asking questions in a chat window instead of clicking through the dashboard, which matters if you already build workflows around assistants.

Are the traffic estimates accurate?

Traffic estimates from every vendor in this category are modelled, not measured, and they differ from each other and from your analytics. Use them for ranking stores against one another rather than as absolute numbers, and verify the method with a store whose real figures you can see.

Is there a free version?

There are free tools rather than a free plan: a competitor analysis report, an ads finder, a product finder, a break-even calculator and a Chrome extension. They are enough to judge data quality, though the saved folders, tracking and email libraries sit behind the paid product.
